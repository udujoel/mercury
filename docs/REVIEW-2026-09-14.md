# Product review — 2026-09-14

Eight independent reviews of Mercury as a thing someone uses at 7am, not as a
codebase: the morning ritual, the content, the desk, what breaks unattended,
cost, the data model, trust, and the gap between what it is and what it is
for. Every finding below was verified against code, live production artefacts
or the live Worker before it was written down; the few that are inference are
marked as such.

The value is not the 49 findings. It is that lenses which never spoke to each
other kept arriving at the same three places.

---

## 1. One ten-line function causes most of the disappointment

`detect_newsletter` (`backend/app/ingestion/gmail.py:184`) returns True for any
mail carrying `List-Unsubscribe`, `Precedence: bulk`, a `List-Id`, or a sender
matching `noreply@` / `no-reply@` / `hello@`.

Every transactional sender on earth sets `List-Unsubscribe`. Five of the eight
reviews, looking at five different things, traced their worst finding back to
this one predicate:

| Lens | What it found |
|---|---|
| content | The inbox segment has six slots and automated mail wins all six |
| desk | 23 of 40 LLM-written story cards go to bank receipts and PR bots |
| data | Every email outranks every news story by more than corroboration can earn |
| trust | Bank balances are on the browse desk, summarised by an LLM, for 30 days |
| trust | A security warning can never be urgent — newsletters are pinned LOW |

Live evidence, from `/briefing/2026-09-13-1835/digest`: of 35 items on the
newsletters shelf, four GTBank alerts, two Wise notices, Revolut, six GitHub
notification mails and seven PR-review bots. The persisted story cards include
"GTBank Debit Transaction Notification" with a balance figure and "Wise Alert
for Scheduled Transfer — EUR 15,766.68". Those bodies were sent to Gemini by
`build_enrichment_prompt` as though they were published journalism.

The guard that was supposed to prevent this exists, and its docstring argues
correctly that private correspondence must not reach a journalism screen. It
then delegates the decision to a header that every bank sets.

**This is the project's named failure mode arriving on the screen where the
stakes are money.** It is also, mercifully, an afternoon of work.

## 2. Personalisation was never connected

`score_stories` accepts `topic_centroid` and `followed_entities`. No caller
passes either (`generate.py:579`). `interest` is therefore the constant 0.5 for
every story, its 0.20 weight cancels out, `urgency` is 0.0 for all news, and
with 153 of 160 stories single-outlet the cluster term is constant too. What
survives is `0.12 × cluster + 0.08 × recency` — a recency sort.

The two RSS stories that reached the 2026-09-13 audio were the two most
recently published in the entire 209-item pool. A Russian strike on a
Ukrainian train, Modi at BRICS and a Fed preview all lost to *"Ask HN: How can
I browse HN in dark mode?"* — by six minutes.

`topic_centroid()` exists, is correct, and is tested. Nobody calls it. A
stranger with the same feed list would receive the identical four stories.

## 3. Two free-tier cliffs are close, and both are silent

**Google Cloud TTS.** Four scheduled generations a day plus the Worker's
checkpoint is ~4.5/day at ~6,300 characters each: roughly 880,000 of the
1,000,000 free Neural2 characters a month, about 88%. Nothing counts them. When
the allowance runs out the run does not fail — `stitch.py:165` drops the turn
and continues, the workflow exits 0, and the watchdog passes because the audio
is still over 1,024 bytes. The briefing shrinks line by line and nothing says
why. Before the six-hourly change (0e4dfec, 2026-09-07) this was 19% of the
tier; nothing recomputed it, and `DEPLOYMENT.md:363` still costs the old
cadence.

**GitHub Actions.** Measured over 2026-08-21 to 09-13, rounding each job up and
applying the 10× macOS multiplier: 1,316 charged minutes, projecting to
1,645/month against a 2,000 cap — and that window mostly predates both of last
week's changes. Priced forward it lands at ~1,950–2,000. The iOS workflow
burned 260 charged minutes on its first day.

The second-order consequence is the serious one: **when Actions minutes run
out, `briefing-watchdog.yml` stops too.** The alarm that exists to say the
briefing is missing is itself an Actions workflow.

Both stale cost comments (`briefing.yml:7`, `DEPLOYMENT.md:27`) say a briefing
costs ~60 minutes a month. Generation alone measures ~325 today.

Meanwhile R2 is at 119 objects and 143 MB — 4% of a 10 GB tier that will not be
reached in a decade. ADR-011's careful deletion-safety work was right; the cost
table pointed at the wrong number.

## 4. The safety gap that survived the fencing

The injection fence (`script_chain.py:332`) tells the model never to repeat a
claim an email makes about the listener's accounts or security. The live
2026-09-13-1835 briefing says, in the follow-ups segment:

> "First, the Afrinvestor login notification. It advises changing your password
> and contacting them if that wasn't you." … "This is a critical security step
> to protect your account."

Fencing stops the model *obeying* an email. It does nothing about the model
faithfully *relaying* one — and relaying is the more dangerous half, because
the hosts strip away the sender address, the headers and the link.

The selection is attacker-controlled: `classify_priority` returns
`(HIGH, requires_response=True)` whenever the user is in To: and the body holds
a "?" or one of eight stock phrases, which a phishing mail satisfies by
construction. Nothing downstream catches it. `check_grounding` scores a claim
against its source text, so **a phishing email's assertions score as more
grounded, not less**, and the critique pass has no category for it.

The fencing itself is the most carefully argued code in the repo and
`net_guard.py` is genuinely good. This is the gap that survived them.

## 5. Four a day was the wrong lever

The cadence change is upstream of most of what is now wrong: it is what put TTS
at 88% and Actions at the cap, it quadrupled publisher-blocking pressure (15% of
article fetches already refused), and it multiplied the repetition — the same
six emails are read out four times a day, and consecutive decks are 86%
identical with no story id surviving between them.

What it bought: the digest carries 209 stories and the audio covers four. A
midday regeneration re-narrates the same four items under a different wire
headline.

And it broke the reliability ladder. The `00:12` cron generates a briefing
named for the *new* day, so all five of the Worker's morning checkpoints see
"this date has a playable briefing" and stand down — including the 05:40 alarm.
Today this is masked only by GitHub running that cron four hours late. After
the 25 October DST change it will not be masked. Meanwhile three of the four
daily slots have no retry, no punctual trigger and no check at all: the
2026-09-13 evening run died with a segfault (exit 139) and nothing reported it.

## 6. The one nobody designed for

**No briefing knows what the last one said.**

Mercury went from one briefing a day to four without acquiring the concept of
"what have I already told you". Every run regenerates from scratch. That single
missing primitive is upstream of the repetition, the 86%-identical decks, the
wasted TTS characters, and the reason a second briefing feels like a worse
version of the first rather than an update to it.

Continuity is the feature the cadence change needed and did not get: *"Since we
last spoke at midday, two things moved."* It is also the cheapest way to make
four-a-day worth its cost — or to discover it is not.

## Worth dropping

- **Task 21 (word timings through the cache).** No briefing has ever shipped
  word timings; ElevenLabs is the only provider that produces them and it is
  401ing every morning.
- **`StoryCards.swift`** — 158 lines nothing renders, including a second copy
  of a count bug already fixed once.
- **Phase 4's new sources and Phase 5's transcript search.** The problem is the
  denominator, not the numerator: 209 stories in, four out.
- **Attention on cross-source dedup.** The whole reason for the shared Item
  schema fires 8 times in 5,590 stories. The code is correct; the premise that
  sources overlap is not.
- **Storage cost.** 4% of tier. Move the cost table onto characters and
  minutes.

## Ranked, by what it buys per hour spent

1. Split the newsletter flag — `is_transactional` for desk eligibility, and an
   escalation list so account warnings can be urgent. Fixes five lenses at once
   and closes the privacy exposure. **S.**
2. Cut the cadence to two a day, and log characters and charged minutes per
   run. Buys back both cliffs. **S.**
3. Set `TTS_PROVIDER=google` in the workflow env. Stops the monthly
   mid-briefing voice swap and a doomed call per run. **S.**
4. Pass `topic_centroid` into `score_stories`. Turns the world segment from a
   recency sort into a ranking. **S.**
5. Freshness floor on the Worker's morning checkpoints, plus a `--max-age-hours`
   watchdog covering all four slots. **M.**
6. A `RELAYED_INSTRUCTION` critique category and gate; have the hosts name the
   sender domain and stop. **M.**
7. Decode `degraded_sources` on the phone. Three mornings in September shipped
   with no inbox at all and the app said nothing. **S.**
8. Continuity between briefings. **M**, and the one with the most upside.
