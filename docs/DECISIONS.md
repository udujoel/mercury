# Architecture Decisions

## ADR-001 — Server generates, phone plays

**Status:** accepted

iOS `BGAppRefreshTask` grants roughly 30 seconds of CPU. Generation takes two
to four minutes. Background windows are also opportunistic, granted based on
usage patterns, battery, thermals and network, with no guarantee.

So: all ingestion, clustering, LLM and TTS work runs server side on a cron. The
finished audio and manifest are pushed to the device by silent notification.
The iOS app is a player with local caching.

Rejected alternative: on-device generation with Apple Foundation Models. Fails
on time budget and on battery, and TTS quality on-device is not competitive.

## ADR-002 — Clean dialogue in the script, naturalness from the TTS model

**Status:** accepted

The NotebookLM team confirmed that the micro-interjections making their audio
sound human are generated by the audio model, not written into the transcript.

So the LLM writes clean dialogue. If output sounds robotic we change the TTS
provider. We never add filler words to the prompt — it makes output worse.

## ADR-003 — Cluster stories, do not summarise articles

**Status:** accepted

Taken from Particle. Without clustering you hear the same story five times
because five newsletters covered it. Clustering is the difference between a
digest that feels edited and one that feels concatenated.

At personal scale (hundreds of items per day) scikit-learn agglomerative
clustering over an in-memory matrix is sufficient. No vector database until
the app serves more than one user.

## ADR-004 — Every AI provider behind a protocol

**Status:** accepted

Free tiers get deprecated and pricing changes. LLM, TTS and embeddings each sit
behind a `Protocol` in `app/core/providers.py`. Swapping a provider means one
new file, never a pipeline change.

## ADR-005 — AVAudioEngine over AVPlayer

**Status:** accepted

Transcript sync displays one large sentence at a time. AVPlayer position
reporting is not precise enough to keep that in sync with speech.
AVAudioEngine gives sample-accurate frame positions.

Cost: we implement seek, rate control and Now Playing integration ourselves.
Worth it — the transcript is the visual composition of the player screen.

## ADR-006 — Google Neural2 now, ElevenLabs next; music is part of the product

**Status:** accepted

The Phase 0 gate passed. Real Google Cloud TTS Neural2 output cleared the ten
second test — Joel's verdict was "the audio is good, but can be better", which
is a pass, not an endorsement.

So Neural2 stays for now and ElevenLabs is the planned upgrade rather than an
emergency swap. That is exactly the case ADR-004 exists for: `TTSProvider` in
`app/core/providers.py` is already satisfied by `GoogleTTSProvider`, so the
migration is one new file in `app/providers/` plus an env change, with no
pipeline edit.

What changes with ElevenLabs, and why it is worth the roughly 22 USD a month:
prosody and disfluency are native to the model, which is precisely the quality
CLAUDE.md rule 1 says we buy from the TTS engine rather than write into the
script. Flash v2.5 is also sub-300ms per turn, which matters for the Phase 3
voice interrupt where the whole latency budget is 2.5 seconds.

**Intro and outro music.** A briefing that starts cold on a voice sounds like a
file playing. A short stinger before the cold open and under the sign-off is
what makes it sound like a show, and it is the cheapest identity the product
can have. Constraints: a few seconds only, ducked well under the voice, and the
timing manifest must account for its duration or every transcript offset shifts
by the length of the intro. That last point is the real engineering content
here — `synthesize_briefing` currently assumes turn zero starts at 0ms.

Rejected: generating music per briefing. It is slow, expensive, non-deterministic
and nobody wants a different theme every morning. Two fixed audio files, chosen
once.
